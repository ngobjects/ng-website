Skoffín

A component-based Java web framework.

Born of a cat and a fox, in Icelandic folklore.

The creature

A skoffín is what you get when a fox fathers kittens on a cat: a creature of the dark, half one thing, half another, and more than either. That suits a framework that pairs an HTML template with a Java class. Monstrous, but the kind you'd still let in. A concept only: nothing here is in the framework.

Cat mother

The face, the slit-pupilled eyes that glow in the dark, the whiskers, the fangs.

Fox father

The tall ears (one of them bitten), the rust ruff and brows, the brush of a tail with its pale tip.

The glare

In the stories, a skoffín's stare kills whatever it falls on. That's the error state.

Silver

Only a gun loaded with silver can stop one. Silver is the colour of the fix: the button that gets you out.

Marks

The head for the logo and the favicon, the tail for the S. At 128, 32 and 16 pixels.

The head

A cat's face under a fox's ears, in the dark. The rust rim keeps it readable on night backgrounds; at 16 it's two eyes and two ears.

The tail

A fox's brush, dark with rust stripes, curled into an S. For the wordmark, and for spots where a face is too much.

Lockups

The wordmark is Fraunces with its soft corners turned all the way up: a storybook serif, a little wonky.
